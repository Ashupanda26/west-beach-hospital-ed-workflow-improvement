# West Beach Hospital: Emergency Department Check-In & Triage Workflow Improvement

**Business Analysis Portfolio Project | 5-Day Simulated Case Study | UK Healthcare Operations**

---

## Project Overview

This simulated Business Analyst portfolio project examines the administrative patient journey within a hospital Emergency Department, from patient arrival and reception through information collection and registration to handover for initial clinical assessment.

The project investigates potential process weaknesses and develops a proposed future-state workflow with clearer responsibilities, structured exception handling, improved handover procedures and defined performance measures.

The case study is based on the supplied West Beach Hospital project briefing. It is an independent portfolio exercise, not a commissioned hospital project or a live NHS implementation.

## Business Problem

The project briefing identifies six potential challenges in the Emergency Department check-in and registration workflow:

1. **Patient Registration Delays:** Administrative steps or incomplete information may delay registration.
2. **Inefficient Information Collection:** Repeated questions, duplicated data entry or missing information may create unnecessary administrative work.
3. **Reception-to-Clinical Handover Challenges:** Unclear communication may affect the transfer of relevant information between teams.
4. **Limited Process Visibility:** Inconsistent recording of arrival and registration milestones may make bottlenecks difficult to identify.
5. **Unclear Administrative Responsibilities:** Unclear task ownership and follow-up procedures may create uncertainty or duplicated effort.
6. **Patient Communication Gaps:** Patients may not receive consistent information about registration requirements or their next steps.

These are potential challenges identified in the project briefing, not verified operational findings.

## Project Objective

To analyse the patient check-in and administrative handover workflow and recommend practical process improvements that support:

- Consistent patient registration.
- Clear administrative responsibilities and accountability.
- Accurate and appropriate information collection.
- Effective reception-to-clinical handover.
- Clear communication of patient next steps.
- Improved visibility of administrative process performance.

## Process Boundary

| Process Boundary | Description |
|---|---|
| **Start Point** | Patient arrival at the Emergency Department |
| **End Point** | Administrative handover to the clinical team for initial clinical assessment |

The investigation covers patient arrival, reception, check-in, information collection, registration, missing-information handling and administrative handover.

**Out of Scope:**
- Clinical assessment and clinical decision-making.
- Changes to clinical triage criteria or patient prioritisation.
- Clinical staffing changes.
- Development or replacement of hospital information systems.
- Software automation or implementation.
- Access to identifiable patient records.
- Live operational implementation and measurement.

The proposed administrative workflow must not delay urgent clinical escalation. Clinical decisions and patient prioritisation remain the responsibility of appropriately authorised clinical staff.

## Proposed Process Improvement Themes

The analysis identifies seven areas for future process improvement:

1. **Standardise Registration:** Define a consistent administrative registration sequence and clear completion criteria.
2. **Improve Information Verification:** Establish minimum information requirements and appropriate verification of existing authorised information.
3. **Clarify Missing-Information Handling:** Assign ownership for incomplete information and outstanding administrative actions.
4. **Standardise Handover:** Define handover information, approved communication routes and acknowledgement procedures.
5. **Improve Process Visibility:** Define arrival, registration and handover milestones and recording responsibilities.
6. **Strengthen Patient Communication:** Clarify responsibility for communicating registration requirements and administrative next steps.
7. **Establish Performance Monitoring:** Introduce a KPI framework to support future administrative process reviews.

These are proposed improvements and have not been implemented or measured in a live hospital environment.

## KPI Framework

Nine proposed KPIs have been defined to support future monitoring of the administrative workflow.

| KPI ID | KPI Name | Measurement Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| KPI-01 | Arrival Acknowledgement Time | Monitor the time between patient arrival and reception acknowledgement. |
| KPI-02 | Registration Processing Time | Monitor the duration of administrative registration activities. |
| KPI-03 | First-Pass Registration Completeness Rate | Assess whether required registration information is complete at the initial check. |
| KPI-04 | Outstanding Information Ownership Rate | Measure whether incomplete information has an assigned owner and follow-up action. |
| KPI-05 | Handover Acknowledgement Rate | Measure the proportion of administrative handovers with confirmed receipt. |
| KPI-06 | Handover Follow-Up Compliance Rate | Monitor follow-up of handovers without confirmed acknowledgement. |
| KPI-07 | Process Milestone Recording Completeness | Assess the completeness of required administrative milestone records. |
| KPI-08 | Patient Next-Step Communication Compliance | Monitor whether patients receive agreed administrative next-step information. |
| KPI-09 | Duplicate Information Request Rate | Assess potentially unnecessary repeated requests for already available authorised information. |

**Measurement approach:** KPI baselines and targets are to be established using authorised operational information and stakeholder agreement. No performance improvement or target percentage is claimed.

## Project Status and Evidence Limitations

This is a simulated Business Analyst portfolio project based on the supplied West Beach Hospital case-study briefing.

The following limitations apply:

- No actual hospital staff interviews have been conducted.
- No live process observations or internal hospital records have been accessed.
- Stakeholder needs and responsibilities are proposed and require organisational validation.
- The current-state process is a scenario-based representation.
- Candidate root causes have not been independently validated.
- No live implementation or software deployment has taken place.
- No measured reduction in registration time, waiting time or administrative workload is claimed.

The proposed workflow and recommendations would require review and approval from relevant operational, clinical and information governance stakeholders before any real-world use.

**Patient safety and clinical accountability:** Administrative registration must not delay urgent clinical escalation. Reception staff do not make clinical decisions, perform clinical triage or determine patient priority.
